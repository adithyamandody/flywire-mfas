Nervous systems are wired less recurrently than their own degree sequences require

Adithya Mandody, independent researcher.

Draft, 27 November 2026. Code and data: https://github.com/adithyamandody/flywire-mfas

Abstract

Order the neurons of a connectome however you like and some synaptic weight still points backward. The minimum over all orderings is a property of the wiring, not of any algorithm — the share of connectivity that no feedforward hierarchy can account for. We measure it with certificates on both sides, a cycle packing below and an exhibited ordering above, in four connectomes spanning two phyla. In Caenorhabditis elegans the two bounds meet exactly, at 3.81%. We first asked whether this quantity grows with nervous-system size, and report that it does not: degree-matched random graphs reproduce the entire pattern, including its non-monotone step, from network density alone. What survives that control is a different result, pointing the opposite way. Compared against random graphs carrying its own degree sequence under the same solver effort, every nervous system we measured is less irreducibly recurrent than chance, by ratios of 0.64, 0.67, 0.79 and 0.90. Zhao and Zhou reported this direction for the 297-node C. elegans network in 2016 [16], against a weaker null; we confirm it with a degree-preserving null, by weight rather than arc count, with certificates, and on connectomes orders of magnitude larger. This holds although the real connectomes carry three to fifty-five times more weight on reciprocated connections than their nulls do — the property that most directly forces backward weight. Real nervous systems are therefore arranged so that their recurrence is unusually resolvable into a hierarchy, not unusually abundant.

1. Introduction

Theories of consciousness disagree about nearly everything except one structural commitment: a feedforward sweep is not sufficient. Recurrent processing theory locates awareness in reentrant signalling [10]; the global neuronal workspace requires ignition sustained by long-range loops [11]; integrated information theory makes irreducibility to independent parts definitional [12]. Each asserts that recurrence matters.

The measurement that bears on this has a precedent. Zhao and Zhou [16] computed near-minimal feedback arc sets for a set of real directed networks, including the 297-node C. elegans network available in 2016, and compared each against an ensemble in which arc directions were randomised. They found real networks markedly feedback-scarce: the worm had 70 feedback arcs where randomisation predicted 405, some 34 standard deviations below chance. The qualitative result in §3.3 below is theirs, and we say so.

Three things have changed since. Whole-brain connectomes now exist at up to 500 times that scale and carry synapse counts, so the question can be asked by weight rather than by arc count. The feedback arc set can be bracketed by certificates rather than approached heuristically, which turns "nearly optimal" into "provably within". And a direction-randomised ensemble is a weak null here, because reversing arcs destroys the degree sequence along with everything else; a degree-preserving null is the harder test, and the one we use.

This paper reports that measurement in four connectomes, and the result of subjecting it to the obvious control. It is a companion to work on a single connectome [14], which established the ordering method on the adult Drosophila brain.

2. The measurement

Given a weighted directed graph and an ordering of its nodes, the backward weight is the total on edges pointing against the order. Minimising it is the weighted minimum feedback arc set problem, which is NP-hard [7]. We do not solve it; we bracket it.

Floor. Assign each directed cycle a value so that the cycles through any edge never exceed its weight. Every directed cycle contains at least one backward edge in every ordering, so the packed total is forced backward always. A packing is a certificate: re-read the graph, check each line is a real cycle, check no edge is over-used.

Ceiling. Any ordering we exhibit achieves some backward rate, and the minimum cannot exceed it. Also a certificate, checkable by re-scoring.

Failure is one-sided. A weak search or a weak packing widens the interval but cannot produce a false claim, only a vague one.

3.2 The raw numbers track density, not size

Our initial hypothesis was that this quantity grows with nervous-system size. It does not. The fourth row already breaks it — the adult whole brain is six times larger than the central brain inside it and reads lower — and the column that orders the rows is connections per cell, not cells.

The decisive test is a degree-preserving null. We rewired each connectome by repeated double-edge swaps, preserving every node's in-degree, out-degree and out-strength and the multiset of edge weights, asserted by re-reading each generated file. Measured the same way, with the same solver effort:

The nulls reproduce the original pattern from density alone, including the non-monotone step that broke the size story: random graphs with hemibrain's degree sequence are more recurrent than random graphs with FlyWire's, exactly as the real ones are, though hemibrain is the smaller brain. Any claim that this quantity scales with nervous-system size is unsupported by these data, and we withdraw it.

Every real nervous system is less irreducibly recurrent than random graphs built from its own degrees. The deficit ranges from 10% to 36%.

This direction is not new. It is Zhao and Zhou's feedback scarcity [16], found in 2016 on the C. elegans network of the day. What is new is how hard the test is. Their null randomised arc directions, which destroys the degree sequence; ours preserves every node's in-degree, out-degree and out-strength, so each null graph is a close relative of the real one. Their effect is correspondingly larger — 70 feedback arcs against 405 — and ours smaller but much harder to explain away. We also measure weight rather than arc count, and the result holds on three connectomes that did not exist when they wrote.

3.4 And it holds against the reciprocity they carry

This is not a consequence of real brains having less of the structure that forces backward weight. They have far more of it. Weight on reciprocated connections, real against null:

systemrealnulls
worm9.9%2.1–3.6%
larva20.6%1.5–1.7%
FlyWire24.9%0.45%
hemibrain41.9%1.7–1.8%

A reciprocated pair is a 2-cycle and forces backward weight directly. Real connectomes carry three to fifty-five times more of it and still finish with less irreducible recurrence than their nulls. Whatever arrangement achieves that has to overcome the reciprocity rather than avoid it.

3.5 How much of this is proved

Only the worm comparison is settled by certificates: every null's floor (4.64–5.24%) exceeds the worm's exact ceiling of 3.81%, so no ordering of any null can be as feedforward as the real worm.

For the other three the comparison is between ceilings at equal solver effort — the same initialiser, search and time budget on real and null alike. That is an estimate, not a proof. It would be wrong only if the solver were systematically several points worse on random graphs than on real ones; replicate spread is 0.16 points or less outside the worm, and the runs were near-flat at their budgets. The floor shortcut is unavailable here because rewiring destroys reciprocity (§3.4), and cycle packing is a weak certificate on a graph with few 2-cycles.

In the adult this is stable across independent solutions: six orderings built from different starting layouts, disagreeing about 2.4% of the graph's weight, place sensory at 7.8 ± 0.1% and motor at 99.8 ± 0.1% [14].

We note what this does not show. An ordering by each neuron's out-share of its own weight, with no optimisation at all, recovers most of this axis [14]. The axis confirms the orderings are not nonsense; it is not evidence that the optimisation found something a simpler statistic could not.

4. Limitations

  • Dataset effects are nearly half the between-system differences. Restricting our FlyWire ordering to FlyWire's own central brain gives a 16.27% ceiling for the tissue where hemibrain certifies an 18.94% floor. Two reconstructions of the same tissue in the same species disagree by at least 2.7 points. Any cross-system difference smaller than about 3 points should be treated as indistinguishable from reconstruction method.
  • The threshold is not a neutral choice. Re-scoring at higher synapse thresholds, the larva runs 13.06% → 5.89% between ≥2 and ≥10 while hemibrain runs 20.84% → 17.07%. The datasets do not respond alike, so fixing a nominal synapse count fixes a number rather than a meaning. The real/null comparison is more robust to this than the raw values, because the null is rebuilt from whatever graph the threshold produces.
  • The denominators differ structurally. Weight outside any strongly connected component can never be backward. That is 50.8% of the worm, 5.0% of the larva and 1.0% of hemibrain, so the raw percentages are fractions of very different things.
  • "297 cells" is not 297 neurons. The C. elegans source lumps the entire musculature into one node, which receives 20.3% of all weight as a pure sink. Removing it, the same certified floor of 262 reads 4.78% rather than 3.81%. The 3.81% is exact for the graph as defined; the graph includes a modelling convenience.
  • Four systems, three of them Drosophila. No trend should be fitted to this. The real/null ratio happens to be monotone in density and is not monotone in size, but with four points that is an observation, not a finding. Mammalian cortex [13] is the point that would matter.
  • This is wiring, not dynamics. Nothing here measures what any circuit computes.

5. What this does and does not say about consciousness

It supplies the theories of §1 with a quantity whose importance they assert but which has been measured only once before, on a 297-node network with a weaker null and without certificates [16]: how much of a nervous system's connectivity is irreducible to any hierarchy, at whole-organism scale.

The direction of the result is worth stating carefully, because it is not the one we expected. These nervous systems are not unusually recurrent for their wiring statistics — they are unusually resolvable, carrying more reciprocity than chance while still admitting a more feedforward arrangement than chance allows. If recurrence is the substrate those theories require, then whatever makes it special is not its quantity, since by that measure brains have less of it than random graphs with the same degrees.

We make no claim that any of these animals is conscious. A fly is not conscious in any settled sense and a worm certainly is not. This is a property of wiring, and a measurement is a precondition for addressing such questions rather than an answer to one.

6. Methods

Graphs. FlyWire from the challenge distribution [1,15]; hemibrain v1.2 traced non-cropped neurons [3]; larva from the published multigraph [4], summing the four compartment-pair edge types; C. elegans from the typed White et al. reconstruction [5,6] as distributed by the OpenWorm Connectome Toolbox, a composite over animals, chemical synapses only. Gap junctions are electrically symmetric and appear as reciprocal pairs no ordering can resolve, manufacturing recurrence from connections that have no direction to be wrong about; a catalogue copy of this connectome that merges them, and whose documentation states its edge directions are undocumented, was discarded after 29% of its reciprocal pairs were found to carry exactly equal weights. Self-loops dropped, connections below two synapses discarded.

Nulls. mfas_code/rewire.py, double-edge swaps at ten attempts per connection, rejecting self-loops and duplicates, weights travelling with their source. Each generated file is re-read and asserted to have identical in- and out-degree per node, identical out-strength per node, and an identical weight multiset. Reciprocal pairs are not forbidden. Seeds are recorded and the nulls are reproducible.

Orderings and bounds. SpringRank initialisation [9] then iterated local search [14]; greedy cycle packing over 2-cycles, 3-cycles and longer cycles by bounded search. Real and null graphs were run through identical pipelines and budgets so the ceilings are comparable.

Verification. Every ordering re-scored by an independent implementation that re-reads graph and ordering from disk and shares no code with the solver; every packing re-verified cycle by cycle. The C. elegans, larval and FlyWire packings are in certificates/.

A correction to [14]. The FlyWire floor reported there as 9.76% came from a packing whose 3-cycle sweep hit its time limit after 108,374 of 136,648 nodes. A complete run packs 5,291,688 units, verified at 1,513,396 cycles with no over-used connection: the floor is 12.63% and the bracket is [12.63%, 15.18%].
