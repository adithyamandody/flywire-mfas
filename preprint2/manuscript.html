How much of a nervous system is irreducibly recurrent? A certified measurement in four connectomes

Adithya Mandody, independent researcher.

Draft, 27 November 2026. Code and data: https://github.com/adithyamandody/flywire-mfas

Abstract

Order the neurons of a connectome however you like and some synaptic weight will still point backward. The minimum of that quantity over all possible orderings is a property of the wiring rather than of any algorithm: the fraction of a nervous system's connectivity that no feedforward hierarchy can account for. We call it irreducible recurrence, and we measure it with certificates on both sides — a cycle packing that lower-bounds it and an explicit ordering that upper-bounds it — in four connectomes spanning two phyla and a 460-fold range in size. In Caenorhabditis elegans the two bounds meet: the packing forces exactly 262 units of synaptic weight backward and an ordering achieves exactly 262, so irreducible recurrence in the worm's chemical connectome is 3.81%, exactly. The whole brain of a Drosophila larva lies in [11.79%, 13.11%], and the adult Drosophila central brain in [18.96%, 20.87%]. These three intervals do not overlap and increase monotonically with size, across 297 to 21,737 cells and three independent reconstructions. Every ordering was computed from wiring alone, and every one recovers the animal's known input-to-output axis — sensory cells first, muscle or motor neurons last — which is evidence that the orderings are not arbitrary members of a large near-optimal set. We report two methodological traps that invert the result if missed: electrical gap junctions are symmetric and manufacture apparent recurrence, and the synapse-count threshold moves the worm's figure from 3.81% to 6.4%.

1. Introduction

Theories of consciousness disagree about nearly everything except one structural commitment: a feedforward sweep is not sufficient. Recurrent processing theory locates awareness in reentrant signalling rather than the initial sweep [10]; the global neuronal workspace requires broadcast and ignition that depend on long-range reentrant loops [11]; integrated information theory makes irreducibility to independent parts the definition of the quantity it proposes [12]. Each asserts that recurrence matters.

None has been accompanied by a measurement of how much recurrence a nervous system actually has, in the sense of wiring that no hierarchy can explain away. Connectomes now make that question answerable. It is not a question about dynamics or about consciousness; it is a question about a directed graph, and it has an exact answer that can be certified.

This paper reports that measurement in four connectomes. It is a companion to work on a single connectome [14], which established the ordering method and its biological interpretation on the adult Drosophila brain; here the object of interest is the bound rather than the ordering, and the comparison across animals rather than within one.

2. The measurement

Given a weighted directed graph G = (V, E, w) and a bijection π: V → {1 … n}, the backward weight is the sum of w(u,v) over edges with π(u) > π(v). Minimising it is the weighted minimum feedback arc set problem, which is NP-hard [7]. We are not trying to solve it. We are trying to bracket its optimum.

Lower bound (floor). Assign every directed cycle C a value y_C ≥ 0 such that for each edge e, the cycles through it satisfy Σ y_C ≤ w_e. Every directed cycle must contain at least one backward edge in any ordering, so the backward weight is at least Σ y_C in every ordering. A packing is a certificate: it can be checked by re-reading the graph, confirming each packed cycle is a genuine directed cycle, and confirming no edge is used beyond its weight.

Upper bound (ceiling). Any ordering we can exhibit achieves some backward rate, and the minimum cannot exceed what is achieved. A heuristic ordering is therefore also a certificate, checkable by re-scoring it.

The two together bracket irreducible recurrence. Note the asymmetry of failure: a weak search or a weak packing only widens the interval. Neither can produce a false claim, only a vague one.

3.2 The worm is solved, not bounded

In C. elegans the cycle packing forces exactly 262 units of synaptic weight backward, and our ordering achieves exactly 262. The bounds meet. Irreducible recurrence in the chemical connectome of the adult hermaphrodite is 3.81%, with no residual uncertainty: no ordering of those 297 cells can do better, and one that attains it is exhibited.

This is, as far as we are aware, the first nervous system for which the quantity is known rather than estimated.

3.3 It rises with size, and the intervals do not overlap

Among the three volumes that are whole nervous systems or central brains — excluding the adult whole brain, for the reason in §3.4 — the measurement increases monotonically and the intervals are disjoint (Figure 1):

A 73-fold range in cell count, two phyla, three laboratories, three reconstruction methods, and a roughly fivefold increase in the share of wiring that no hierarchy explains. Because the intervals are disjoint, the ordering of the three does not depend on where within each interval the truth lies.

3.4 The whole adult brain reads lower than the central brain it contains

The adult whole brain sits at [9.76%, 15.18%], below the central brain that is a subset of it. This is dilution rather than contradiction: the whole brain includes the optic lobes, roughly 57% of its neurons, which are large, repetitive and strongly feedforward. The same effect appears within a single dataset: in the adult, the outbound visual route is a few per cent backward while the return routes are tens of per cent [14]. A measurement of this quantity is therefore a measurement of a volume, and comparisons must say which volume they mean.

3.5 Two ways to get this badly wrong

Gap junctions. Electrical synapses are symmetric. A gap junction appears in a directed graph as a pair of edges of identical weight in opposite directions, which no ordering can ever make forward. Including them manufactures irreducible recurrence out of connections that have no direction to be wrong about. The widely used catalogue copy of the C. elegans connectome merges gap junctions with chemical synapses, and its own documentation states that the meaning of its edge directions is not recorded; 29% of its reciprocal pairs carry exactly equal weights, which is the signature. We discarded it and used the typed White et al. source [5], chemical synapses only.

Threshold. At a one-synapse threshold rather than two, the worm reads [6.29%, 6.41%] instead of 3.81%. Weak connections carry a disproportionate share of the irreducible recurrence. Absolute values of this quantity are therefore threshold-relative, and no comparison across connectomes is meaningful unless the threshold is held fixed, as it is in §3.1.

3.6 The orderings are not arbitrary

A near-optimal ordering is not unique, which raises the question of whether anything read from one is an artifact of which one. Two checks.

First, each ordering recovers the animal's known axis, from wiring alone, with no labels supplied:

firstmiddlelast
C. eleganssensory 24.5%interneuron 42.1%, motor 62.3%muscle 82.5%

D. adultsensory 7.9%optic 47.0%, central 85.5%motor 99.8%

Second, in the adult we built six orderings from different starting layouts with no merging between them. They disagree about the direction of some 1.2 million units of weight — 2.4% of the graph — while scoring within 0.06% of each other. Across all six, sensory neurons sit at 7.8 ± 0.1% and motor neurons at 99.8 ± 0.1%. The axis is a property of the connectome, not of the particular ordering found. Finer classes are not all so stable: Kenyon cells move by about twenty points between orderings, and no claim here rests on the position of any individual class.

4. Limitations

  • Four points, three of them Drosophila. This is not a phylogenetic analysis and no trend should be fitted to it. The informative next point is mammalian cortex [13].
  • Size is confounded with complexity, with developmental stage, and with reconstruction completeness: larger volumes are also more recently and more thoroughly traced.
  • The adult whole-brain interval is wide at 5.42 points. Exact minimum feedback arc set on disjoint induced subgraphs — a strictly stronger bound than cycle packing on the same edges — improves it by only 0.031 points, so the limitation is the relaxation rather than its implementation. Narrowing it needs a different relaxation entirely.
  • "Whole brain" differs between animals. The larval brain excludes the ventral nerve cord; the hemibrain is one hemisphere of a central brain; the C. elegans figure covers the somatic nervous system including muscle targets. We compare what each dataset contains and say so.
  • This is wiring, not dynamics. Nothing here measures what any circuit computes, and a structural measure of recurrence does not establish that the recurrence is functionally used.

5. What this does and does not say about consciousness

It gives the theories in §1 a quantity they have asserted the importance of but never had measured: how much of a nervous system's connectivity is irreducible to any hierarchy, with a certificate, at whole-organism scale. That it rises steeply and monotonically with nervous system size, across two phyla, is the kind of structural fact those theories would predict.

It does not show that any of these animals is conscious, and we make no such claim. A fly is not conscious in any settled sense and a worm is certainly not. What we report is a property of wiring. Whether that property is the substrate those theories require is exactly the open question, and a measurement is a precondition for addressing it rather than an answer to it.

6. Methods

Graphs. FlyWire from the challenge distribution [1,15]; hemibrain v1.2 traced non-cropped neurons [3]; larva from the published multigraph [4], summing the four compartment-pair edge types (ad, aa, da, dd) so that weights mean the same thing they do in the other datasets; C. elegans from the typed White et al. reconstruction [5,6], chemical synapses only. Self-loops dropped, connections below two synapses discarded.

Orderings. A SpringRank initialisation [9] followed by iterated local search over a sifting neighbourhood with hub moves and topological-shuffle kicks. The search is not the contribution and is described in [14].

Bounds. Greedy cycle packing on residual capacities: all 2-cycles, then 3-cycles, then longer cycles by bounded search. Every packing is written out and re-verified by a separate program that re-reads the graph, checks each cycle is a genuine directed cycle, and checks no connection is used beyond its weight. A bound that fails this check is not reported.

Verification. Every ordering is re-scored by an independent pure-stdlib implementation that re-reads the graph and ordering from disk and shares no code with the solver. Both checks run on public data in minutes.
